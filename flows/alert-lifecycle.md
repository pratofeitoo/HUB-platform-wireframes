# Alert lifecycle

```text
Generated -> Unassigned -> In progress -> Decision recorded -> Action tracked -> Closed
                                      |-> Escalated
                                      |-> Suppressed
```

## User and goal

- User: HUB Manager or authorized Manager.
- Goal: move a governed alert from generation to a human-owned, auditable outcome.

## Entry point

An eligible alert is generated by a versioned rule and appears in the authorized Alerts and Decisions queue.

## Steps

1. Review alert identity, severity, affected object, source freshness, and SLA.
2. Assign the alert or leave it unassigned with a visible reason.
3. Inspect explanation, add human context, and record a decision.
4. Create and assign a linked action when action is appropriate.
5. Track SLA and evidence until the alert is closed.

## Decision and error branches

- Escalate when the owner cannot resolve the alert or the SLA is at risk.
- Suppress only with a human reason, rule/status context, and recovery path.
- Keep the alert open when data is stale, partial, or awaiting Finance validation; show the data condition.
- Integration or authorization errors preserve the alert history and route to the relevant recovery or permission state.

## Permission and LGPD checks

- Enforce tenant, organization, hierarchy, role, and object-level access before showing affected data.
- Mask sensitive fields in the queue, detail view, export, and accessibility labels.
- Require valid purpose, legal basis, and consent controls where the canonical source marks data sensitive.

## Endpoint

The alert reaches `Closed` with a linked decision/action and documented outcome, or remains in an explicitly visible `Escalated`, `Suppressed`, or `Awaiting Finance validation` state.

## Audit events

Alert generated, viewed, assigned, context added, decision recorded, action created, owner assigned, escalated, suppressed, Finance status changed, and closed.

## Validation criteria

- Every transition identifies actor, timestamp, reason where applicable, and resulting status.
- No critical alert disappears because its source is stale.
- The same alert ID remains linked to decision, action, evidence, and value records.
