# Employee development flow

`My Journey -> Goal detail -> Check-in -> Feedback -> Skills -> Development plan -> Contest or correct information`

## User and goal

- User: Employee; supporting roles: Manager, RH, and authorized People Analytics reviewer.
- Goal: understand personal progress and development while retaining control over correction and contestation.

## Entry point

The employee opens `/me` for the current cycle, project, or development plan.

## Steps

1. Review goals, status, period, source, and authorized financial/KPI linkage.
2. Open goal detail and submit a check-in.
3. Review or respond to feedback and identify skills or development needs.
4. Review or update the development plan.
5. Ask why, correct, or contest inaccurate data or recommendations.

## Decision and error branches

- Missing or stale data is explained and remains distinguishable from zero performance.
- A manager or RH review may request clarification, but cannot silently overwrite the employee’s correction path.
- A recommendation may be accepted, modified, or rejected by a human; it cannot autonomously determine promotion, punishment, or dismissal.
- Restricted data routes to a permitted aggregate or self-only view.

## Permission and LGPD checks

- The employee sees their own authorized record; managers and RH see only policy-approved scope.
- Sensitive demographic or health-related fields are not shown at individual level unless explicitly authorized by policy and legal basis.
- Preserve purpose, consent, revocation, retention, and audit controls.

## Endpoint

The employee has a current check-in and development plan, or a tracked correction/contestation case with a response owner and SLA.

## Audit events

Journey viewed, goal viewed/updated, check-in submitted, feedback recorded, skill/development plan changed, contestation opened, correction applied, and response notified.

## Validation criteria

- Source and freshness are visible for relevant information.
- Employee correction and contestation are reachable without exposing another person’s data.
- Human ownership is explicit for feedback, recommendations, and consequential decisions.

The employee must see the source of relevant data and have a clear contestation path.
